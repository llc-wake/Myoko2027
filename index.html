What to focus on before January Important

With five months to go and no snow available, the honest priority is off-snow physical preparation, because that is the only thing you can actually change between now and December.

  • Single-leg strength and balance — snowboarding punishes weak glutes and ankles. Split squats, step-downs, calf raises.
  • Rotational core control — your turns come from the hips, not the shoulders. Pallof press, cable rotations, dead bugs.
  • Sustained low-crouch endurance — wall sits and isometric holds. Day 3 fatigue is what breaks technique.
  • Ankle mobility — dorsiflexion range directly limits how much you can drive a toe edge.
  • Falling practice, seriously — learning to go to forearms and roll rather than catching with a straight wrist prevents the single most common beginner injury.

Your existing tennis and gym base is a genuine advantage here — the transferable gap is usually rotational control and single-leg endurance rather than general fitness.

Suggested drills Watch
  • Drill 1 — Bulgarian split squat, 3 × 8 each leg, twice weekly. Builds the single-leg strength that lets you hold an edge instead of skidding.
  • Drill 2 — Pallof press, 3 × 12 each side. Trains resisting rotation, which is exactly what your core does through a carve.
  • Drill 3 — Wall sit progression, 3 × 45–90 s. Directly maps to the riding stance you will hold for hours on day 3.
  • Bonus — balance board or slackline, 10 min, three times weekly. Cheapest, highest-transfer purchase you can make before this trip.

Gear & safety notes Important

Priority order for two beginners: helmet → impact shorts → wrist guards. Certified helmet only — look for ASTM F2040 or CE EN1077 on the label.

Avalanche equipment is not required for this trip as planned. Akakura, Ikenotaira and Suginohara are all lift-served, in-bounds, patrolled terrain. It only becomes relevant if you hire a guide for sidecountry, which is not appropriate at your level this season.

Boot fitting is still the highest-leverage unfinished item.
